### 00_hook
Nineteen twelve. A train rolls out of Miami, and heads for the sea. It does not stop at the edge of the land. It keeps going, over bridges and causeways, across the open water, for more than one hundred miles, until it reaches Key West. Engineers had called it impossible, and newspapers had called it Flagler's folly.

At the front of the first train, an old man is helped to a seat. He is eighty-two years old. He has been rich for decades, and he has spent a fortune to build this one railroad.

His name is Henry Flagler. And this is the story of the partner of Rockefeller who built Florida.

### 01_cap1
Henry Morrison Flagler was born on January second, eighteen thirty, in Hopewell, New York. His father was a poor Presbyterian minister. When Henry was fourteen, he left school after the eighth grade, and walked out of the house with almost nothing.

He went to Ohio, to work in the general store of his relatives, the Harkness family. He earned five dollars a month, plus room and board. He worked hard, and he learned how trade worked. After a while he became a partner in a grain business, and he married Mary Harkness, a member of the same family.

In his early years, his business grew, and then it collapsed. After the Civil War, he invested in a salt business in Michigan, and it failed. He was left in debt, and his reputation was damaged.

### 02_cap2
In Cleveland, he started again as a grain merchant. And there he met a young bookkeeper from the same town, named John D. Rockefeller. They had known each other from the grain business, and they became friends.

In eighteen sixty-seven, Rockefeller needed money to grow his oil refining company. Flagler's relative, Stephen Harkness, provided the capital, and Flagler joined as a partner. The firm was called Rockefeller, Andrews, and Flagler.

In eighteen seventy, they turned the company into Standard Oil. Flagler became its secretary and treasurer. He was a master negotiator, and he made secret deals with the railroads for lower shipping prices, which helped Standard Oil crush its rivals.

### 03_cap3
By the late eighteen seventies, Flagler was already a rich man. But his wife Mary was sick, and the doctors recommended a warm climate. In eighteen seventy-eight, the couple spent a winter in Jacksonville, Florida.

Mary died in eighteen eighty-one. Two years later, he married Ida Alice Shourds. On their honeymoon, they visited St. Augustine, the old Spanish city on the Florida coast. Flagler was fascinated. The sun was warm, the beaches were beautiful, but the hotels were poor and the trains were slow.

He saw a business. If he could build the hotels, and the railroad to reach them, wealthy northerners would come in winter. He decided to build it himself.

### 04_cap4
In eighteen eighty-five, Flagler began building the Ponce de Leon Hotel in St. Augustine, a palace in the Spanish style, with towers and courtyards. It opened in eighteen eighty-eight, and it was among the first buildings in America built with poured concrete. Famous artists decorated it, including Louis Comfort Tiffany.

He then bought small railroads along the coast, and rebuilt them into one line, which became the Florida East Coast Railway. As the tracks moved south, new hotels and towns appeared along them.

In eighteen ninety-four, the railroad reached Palm Beach, and Flagler opened the huge Royal Poinciana Hotel. In eighteen ninety-six, the tracks reached a small settlement beside Biscayne Bay. The people there asked him to name it after himself, but he said no. The town became Miami.

### 05_cap5
Flagler then had his boldest idea. He wanted to extend the railroad across the sea, to Key West, the closest deepwater port to the Panama Canal, which was being built. The project began in nineteen oh five.

It was one of the hardest jobs ever attempted. Workers built concrete bridges across open water, and they fought mosquitoes, heat, and hurricanes. A storm in nineteen oh six killed many workers. Skeptics laughed at the old man's folly, but Flagler kept paying, and kept building.

On January twenty-second, nineteen twelve, the first train arrived in Key West, with Flagler on board. Crowds cheered. The Overseas Railroad had cost about fifty million dollars in total, a fortune.

### 06_cap6
Flagler's private life brought sorrow. His second wife Ida became mentally ill, and he had her committed. His son Harry, from his first marriage, was his only surviving child. In nineteen oh one, he married a third time, to Mary Lily Kenan, and he built a marble mansion called Whitehall in Palm Beach as a wedding gift.

In January of nineteen thirteen, he fell down a flight of stairs at Whitehall, and never recovered. He died on May twentieth, nineteen thirteen, at the age of eighty-three.

His railroad was damaged by a hurricane in nineteen thirty-five, and later became a highway. But the cities he built, Palm Beach and Miami, and the tourism industry of Florida, remain his monument.

### 07_lesson
Flagler teaches that a second act can be bigger than the first. He built Standard Oil with Rockefeller until he was in his fifties, and then he put his fortune into a vision that others could not see.

He looked at a place that nobody wanted, and he imagined what it could become. Then he built the roads, and the rails, to get there. The people who change the map are the ones who build the infrastructure first.
