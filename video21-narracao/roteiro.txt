### 00_hook
September twenty-eighth, two thousand eight. A man stands in a control room in California, watching a live feed of a rocket climb into the sky over the Pacific Ocean. The last three launches of his company all ended in explosions. His money is almost gone. His electric car company is days away from bankruptcy. If this fourth rocket fails, both companies die, and so does his dream.

The rocket reaches orbit. Three months later, NASA gives him a contract worth one point six billion dollars. And on Christmas Eve, he saves his car company with a last-minute deal.

His name is Elon Musk. And this is the story of the man who bet everything, twice, and won.

### 01_cap1
Elon Reeve Musk was born on June twenty-eighth, nineteen seventy-one, in Pretoria, South Africa. His mother, Maye, was a model and a dietitian. His father, Errol, was an engineer. The marriage ended when Elon was about ten years old, and he went to live with his father, a choice he would later describe as a hard one.

He was a quiet, bookish child, who read for hours, sometimes ten hours a day. At about ten, he taught himself to program a computer. At twelve, he wrote a video game called Blastar, and sold the code to a magazine for about five hundred dollars.

School was painful. He was bullied badly, and once he was beaten so severely that he ended up in the hospital. He wanted out. At seventeen, he left South Africa for Canada, with almost no money, to find his mother's relatives.

### 02_cap2
In Canada, he worked odd jobs. He cleaned out a boiler room. He worked on a farm. Then he studied at Queen's University, and later moved to the University of Pennsylvania, where he earned two degrees, one in economics and one in physics.

In nineteen ninety-five, he was accepted for a doctorate at Stanford. He lasted two days. The internet was exploding, and he did not want to watch it from a classroom. He quit, and started a company with his younger brother, Kimbal.

The company was called Zip2. It built online city guides and maps for newspapers. The brothers slept in their small office and showered at the YMCA. In nineteen ninety-nine, Compaq bought Zip2 for about three hundred and seven million dollars. Elon was twenty-seven, and his share was about twenty-two million dollars.

### 03_cap3
Most people would have rested. Musk put almost all of the money into a new idea, an online bank called X dot com. In two thousand, it merged with a rival company, and became PayPal. Musk was pushed out as chief executive while he was on a flight to a honeymoon, but he kept his shares.

In two thousand two, eBay bought PayPal for one point five billion dollars. Musk's share was about one hundred and seventy-five million dollars. He was thirty-one years old, and he was rich.

Then he did something that his friends thought was crazy. He decided to build rockets. He had read that a rocket was thousands of times cheaper to build than it was to buy, because almost everything was thrown away after one use. He wanted to land them and use them again.

### 04_cap4
In March of two thousand two, he founded SpaceX, and put in about a hundred million dollars of his own money. Experts laughed. The engineers he hired were young, and the budget was small.

In two thousand four, he also invested in a little car company called Tesla, and became its chairman. Soon he was running both companies, and paying for both.

Then the failures began. The first Falcon 1 rocket, in two thousand six, burned up seconds after liftoff. The second, in two thousand seven, did not reach orbit. The third, in August of two thousand eight, failed too. The financial crisis hit at the same moment. Tesla was running out of cash, and Musk was running out of his own. He later said that he had money for just one more try.

### 05_cap5
That was the launch on September twenty-eighth, two thousand eight. Falcon 1 reached orbit, and became the first privately built liquid-fuel rocket to do it. Two months later, NASA awarded SpaceX a contract worth one point six billion dollars to carry cargo to the International Space Station.

Days before Christmas, Tesla was almost out of cash. On Christmas Eve, two thousand eight, investors agreed to put in new money, and Tesla survived by only a few days.

From there, the climb began. Tesla went public in two thousand ten. In two thousand twelve, a SpaceX capsule docked with the space station. In December of two thousand fifteen, a Falcon 9 rocket landed upright after reaching space, something that many experts had called impossible.

### 06_cap6
The road stayed rough. In two thousand seventeen and two thousand eighteen, Tesla struggled to build its Model 3, and Musk slept at the factory. In two thousand twenty, Tesla became the most valuable car company in the world. In January of two thousand twenty-one, Musk became the richest person on Earth.

In October of two thousand twenty-two, he bought Twitter for forty-four billion dollars, a deal that drew as much criticism as praise. He also kept pushing Starship, a giant rocket meant to carry people to Mars.

He is a controversial man, and his critics are many. But few people in history have started or transformed so many industries, and few have risked so much money while doing it.

### 07_lesson
The lesson is simple, and it is not easy. Musk risked everything he had, not once, but twice, on ideas that most people called impossible. He did not win because he had no fear. He won because he kept going after three explosions, when quitting would have been reasonable.

If a goal is worth chasing, expect failures, and decide in advance that they are not the end. The ones who win are often the ones who are still in the game after everyone else has left.
